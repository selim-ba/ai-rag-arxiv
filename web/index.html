Overview

A question-answering system over 49 arXiv papers on world models and joint-embedding predictive architectures. It searches the papers, answers only from what it found, and refuses when they do not contain the answer. Every number below is measured against a hand-written test set — what each one means.

How a question is answered

  • Search — the papers are split into 874 chunks; two searches run at once, one by meaning and one by exact words, and the rankings are merged.
  • Answer — written only from the five best-ranked chunks it found, with citations filled in from the search results so they cannot be invented.
  • Or refuse — when the chunks do not support an answer.

Finding the right chunk

Measured on a hand-written test set of 40 questions: 34 with a known answer, where the chunks that really contain it were labelled by hand, and 6 that the papers deliberately cannot answer. The table below uses the 34. Five chunks are retrieved per question, and each chunk is stored as a 1,536-dimensional embedding (OpenAI text-embedding-3-small) for meaning-based search, and in a word index for exact-term search.

how it searches hit@1 hit@5 recall@5
by meaning only0.3240.6180.520
by exact words only0.3240.4710.402
both, weighted equally0.3820.6470.534
both, tuned (shipped)0.3530.6760.578

Combining the two searches naively gained nothing — it won four questions and lost five, because the merge rewards chunks both searches agree on and buries one that only a single search found. The fix was to weight them: each search contributes a score of 1/(5 + its rank), and the word-search side is multiplied by 0.3 before the two are added. The weaker search still breaks ties and still rescues the questions only it can find, without outvoting the stronger one. The 0.3 was chosen on a development split from a grid of 30 combinations.

Known limits

  • Finding the right chunk is the bottleneck. It succeeds about two-thirds of the time, and nothing after it can recover from a miss.
  • The corpus is fixed at 49 papers, so anything outside them gets a refusal. Adding live web search, so questions beyond the corpus could be answered from sources it fetches and cites, is the obvious next piece of work.
  • Retrying with a reworded question does not help here. When the grader judges the chunks insufficient, rewording and searching again rescued one question across two runs and lost two. Built, tested, and switched off.
  • There is no verification pass over the answer. The plan was a second call to the same model (gpt-4o-mini, the one that writes the answers) to check the first. Measuring the problem it was meant to fix came first, and the problem turned out not to exist: the single “failure” it targeted was a correct refusal phrased without the word the scoring script looked for. Never built.
  • This is a personal research project: built, measured, documented and deployed to learn how retrieval-augmented generation works and to read the world-model literature properly at the same time. Suggestions and improvements are very welcome.
